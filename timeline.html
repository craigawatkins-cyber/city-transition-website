What's next, and when

This timeline is illustrative, not committed — it's the sequence the feasibility study proposes if Holiday Island decides to move forward. Every stage has an off-ramp built in: if a vote or a petition fails, that piece of the transition simply pauses. Jump straight to why the November sales-tax vote matters most →

2026 — Roads & the sales tax question

  • Prepare legal documents to transition the Road Department to the city.
  • Transition the Road Department.
  • Submit an ordinance to the county clerk placing a 2.5% sales tax on the November ballot.
  • Election on the sales tax question. If it fails, further transition planning is put on hold.

2027 — Water, fire, and the public safety petition

  • Once the sewer bond is retired, transition the Water Department.
  • Decide whether to pursue a police department or a sheriff/deputy agreement — see the Public Safety page.
  • Petition to form a Public Safety Urban Service District (325 signatures needed). If it fails, this transition is put on hold.
  • If successful, the City Council establishes the Public Safety USD and planning begins for the Fire Department transition.

2028 — Parks, recreation, and HISID dissolution

  • Petition to form a Parks & Recreation Urban Service District. If it fails, this transition is put on hold.
  • If successful, plan and transition recreational amenities.
  • HISID begins its formal dissolution process.

Why the November vote is the hinge point

Every later stage of this plan — the water transition, the public safety petition, the parks petition, HISID's dissolution — sits downstream of one election. If the 2.5% sales tax fails in November, the study is explicit: "further transition planning is put on hold." That's not a footnote. It's the reason this vote deserves more attention than a routine ballot question would.

If it passes

Roughly $502,660 a year begins flowing into the General Fund — a meaningful piece of replacing the assessment revenue HISID can no longer safely guarantee. It's also the one new revenue source in this whole plan that Holiday Island shares with everyone who spends money here, not just property owners: weekend visitors, contractors, delivery drivers, and day-trippers passing through all pay a share too. The rest of the 2026–2028 timeline — water, public safety, parks, and an orderly HISID wind-down — stays on track, on a schedule the community chose for itself.

If it fails

The plan doesn't vanish — it pauses. But the legal and financial pressure that started this conversation doesn't pause with it. HISID's funding structure is the same one that's already produced a dissolved SID in Horseshoe Bend, a court-ordered dissolution deadline in Cherokee Village, and an active lawsuit right here in Holiday Island. Waiting doesn't remove that risk; it just hands the timing and the terms to a judge instead of to residents. See why this matters for the full picture.

There's a longer-term stake too. A working sales tax base is also the foundation of Holiday Island's growth plan — every dollar it raises scales automatically with every new resident who chooses to move here afterward, with no additional rate increase required. Passing it in November isn't only about balancing next year's budget; it's what lets the next fifty years of growth help pay for itself.

The choice, in the study's own words

Option #1: Pursue this or a similar plan. "It's better to have our plan than to have someone else's plan forced on us."

Option #2: Transition only road responsibility in 2026, then wait to see how HISID's long-term legal situation resolves on its own.

Both are honest options on the table. This site exists because we believe Option #1 — shaping our own plan deliberately, on our own timeline — is the better path, given what's already happened to Horseshoe Bend, Cherokee Village, and Ozark Acres. But that's ultimately a decision for the whole community to make together, through votes and petitions, not something anyone can decide alone.

How to make your voice count

Get informed

Read through the Why This Matters, Budget, and Services pages, and try the Cost Calculator with your own numbers.

Show up

Attend City Council and HISID Board of Commissioners meetings where these decisions are discussed. Public comment matters, especially on a decision this significant.

Sign when petitions circulate

The Public Safety and Parks & Recreation Urban Service Districts can only be created if enough registered voters sign a petition. If you want either to happen, your signature is what makes it possible.

Vote

The 2.5% sales tax question is expected on the November 2026 ballot — the single most important date on this whole timeline. Whatever you decide, make sure your voice is counted.
