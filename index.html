Holiday Island is at a fork in the road. Let's choose it on purpose.

For over 50 years, Holiday Island's water, sewer, roads, fire protection, and recreation have been run by the Suburban Improvement District (HISID) — a structure built to fund new infrastructure, not to maintain it forever. Nearby sister communities are finding that out the hard way. Here's what a transition to full city services would mean for you, in plain language.

See what it could cost me → Why this matters now

This November, Holiday Island votes on its own future

A 2.5% sales tax is expected on the ballot — the single revenue source this entire plan depends on. If it passes, roughly $502,660 a year starts replacing the assessment revenue HISID can no longer safely guarantee, on a timeline Holiday Island chooses for itself. If it fails, the feasibility study is explicit: "further transition planning is put on hold" — and the legal and financial pressure that started this conversation doesn't pause with it, the way it hasn't for three neighboring communities already. Between now and November: get informed, talk with your neighbors, make sure you're registered, and vote.

It's already happening around us

Three neighboring Arkansas improvement districts have already been forced into court-driven change. Holiday Island has settled one lawsuit already and is in another right now.

What's happened to similar Arkansas improvement districts
CommunityWhat happened
Horseshoe BendSID dissolved after a lawsuit; the city took over recreational amenities in 2025.
Ozark AcresSettled a lawsuit by moving toward incorporating and phasing out its SID by 2028.
Cherokee VillageA judge's order requires the SID to be fully dissolved by the end of 2026.
Holiday IslandSettled one lawsuit in 2011; currently involved in another that is still being litigated.

None of these districts set out to break the law — they simply ran into a funding structure that was never designed to last forever. Read the full picture on why this matters →

A place worth investing in

Whatever happens with this transition, Holiday Island's appeal doesn't change: an Ozark lake, a marina, two golf courses, pools, trails, and a community that has always punched above its weight on recreation and lifestyle.
