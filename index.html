Holiday Island is at a fork in the road. Let's choose it on purpose.

Holiday Island has moved from studying whether a transition is feasible to planning how it would happen. The City and HISID have endorsed a Long Range Plan that sets the framework for moving services from HISID to the City and eventually phasing out HISID. Here is what that plan says, what it could cost you, and what is still undecided, in plain language.

See the Long Range Plan → How it would be funded

What's changed?

On September 22, 2026, the City Council and the HISID Board of Commissioners endorsed a five-phase Long Range Plan. The first phase is decided: the City takes over road maintenance on January 1, 2027. Water and sewer, public safety and fire, and recreation are planned to follow, ending with the phase-out of HISID. See the timeline →

But the financial and legal details still matter

The Long Range Plan is an endorsed framework, not a binding contract, and the plan says so itself. Individual phases still require a sales tax vote, annual budgets, petitions, a bond payoff, legal actions and other public processes. See what is decided and what is still open →

On November 3, Holiday Island votes on the next step

A proposed 2.5% City sales tax is on the November 3, 2026 ballot. It is a ballot measure, not an adopted tax. Here is what the Long Range Plan says about it:

  • What it is for. The plan intends it to cover the added administrative cost the City takes on as HISID's functions move over, about $437,000 a year1. It is one part of the funding framework, alongside property tax, water and sewer rates, and Urban Service District fees.
  • If it passes, planning for the water and sewer and fire department transitions can begin. It does not complete or guarantee them; each still has its own requirements.
  • If it fails, future transition planning is put on hold, those services stay with HISID, and the assessment is not reduced beyond the road transfer. Roads move to the City on January 1, 2027 either way.

Source: Long Range Plan, September 22, 2026, pages 5–6. The earlier May 2026 feasibility study estimated the tax would raise about $502,660 a year2, based on 2,452 residents and $82 per person for each 1% of tax. That is a projection, not future revenue anyone can promise.

It's already happening around us

Three other Arkansas improvement districts have already been pushed into change by lawsuits. Holiday Island has settled one lawsuit already and is in another right now.

What's happened to similar Arkansas improvement districts2
CommunityWhat happened
Horseshoe BendSID dissolved after a lawsuit; the city took over recreational amenities in 2025.
Ozark AcresSettled a lawsuit by moving toward incorporating and phasing out its SID by 2028.
Cherokee VillageA judge ordered the SID dissolved by the end of 2026. A settlement in a second lawsuit has since given the district about two more years to work out a plan; the underlying issue remains.
Holiday IslandSettled one lawsuit in 2011; currently involved in another that is still being litigated.

Source: May 2, 2026 feasibility study presentation.

Three things to understand

Why this matters

HISID's assessment model was built to create infrastructure, not to maintain it indefinitely. The Long Range Plan projects that, if nothing changes, HISID reaches the end of what it can assess in 20371.

What will this cost?

Every resident's situation is different — a vacant lot, a modest home, a lake-view retirement house. The Long Range Plan describes the HISID assessment stepping down phase by phase while City taxes and fees phase in. Most of the dollar amounts will be set in future budgets. A place worth investing in

Whatever happens with this transition, Holiday Island's appeal doesn't change: an Ozark lake, a marina, two golf courses, pools, trails, and a community that has always punched above its weight on recreation and lifestyle. This conversation is about making sure that stays true for the next fifty years, not just the next five — and about growing on purpose, so more residents can share in it. Holiday Island also sits next door to the NWA corridor, the 9th fastest-growing metro area in the country,3 which makes it a natural choice for retirees and remote workers, and makes a stable city structure worth getting right.
