Why this matters

Holiday Island became a city in 2020.1 Since then, the City and HISID have each kept providing different services. The Long Range Plan is the framework for bringing those services together over time.

One community. One government. One clear place to be accountable.

On November 3, Holiday Island votes on the next step

A proposed 2.5% City sales tax is on the November 3, 2026 ballot. It is a ballot measure, not an adopted tax. Here is what the Long Range Plan says about it:

  • What it is for. The ballot measure would authorize a 2.5% local sales and use tax for general fund expenses and capital improvements.2 The Long Range Plan identifies the added administrative cost of consolidation as the principal reason for it, about $437,000 a year1. It is one part of the funding framework, alongside property tax, water and sewer rates, and Urban Service District fees.
  • If it passes, planning for the water and sewer and fire department transitions can begin. It does not complete or guarantee them; each still has its own requirements.
  • If it fails, future transition planning is put on hold, those services stay with HISID, and the assessment is not reduced beyond the road transfer. Roads move to the City on January 1, 2027 either way.

Source: Long Range Plan, September 22, 2026, pages 5–6. The earlier May 2026 feasibility study estimated the tax would raise about $502,660 a year3, based on 2,452 residents and $82 per person for each 1% of tax. That is a projection, not future revenue anyone can promise.

What happens next?

  • October 2026: the City is asking residents for comments on the Long Range Plan.4
  • November 3, 2026: voters decide on a proposed 2.5% City sales tax.2
  • 2027: the Long Range Plan calls for the City to assume 100% of road maintenance responsibility beginning January 1, 2027.1 Endorsed / Planned
  • 2027 to 2029: water and sewer, public safety and fire, and recreation are planned to follow. Planned
  • 2030 and later: the phase-out of HISID, with no date set.

The Long Range Plan is an endorsed framework, not a guarantee of every future step. Later steps may depend on votes, annual budgets, petitions, paying off the sewer bond, legal actions, and future City and HISID decisions.
