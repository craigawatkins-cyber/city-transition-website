About this site

Holiday Island's Future is an independent, resident-built community education project. It is not an official publication of the City of Holiday Island, HISID, or any government body.

Why this site exists

In 2026 the City of Holiday Island and the Holiday Island Suburban Improvement District (HISID) moved from studying whether the City could take over HISID's services to endorsing a Long Range Plan for doing so. It's a complex, numbers-heavy topic spread across a plan document and two long presentations — easy for residents to miss, and hard to fully absorb in a single public meeting. This site exists to make that same information easier to find, easier to read, and easier to apply to your own household, while making an honest case for why the author believes following the plan through is worth it.

The site began in mid-2026, when only the feasibility presentations existed. It was revised in October 2026 so that the Long Range Plan is its foundation.

Sources

Sources are grouped by how much weight they carry. When they conflict, the most recent official source should be given priority.

Primary source

  • City of Holiday Island & Holiday Island Suburban Improvement District, Long Range Plan, dated September 22, 2026 (Resolution 2026-015, 8 pages). Endorsed by the Holiday Island City Council and the HISID Board of Commissioners. Read the official plan, linked from the "View Long Range Plan" button on the City's homepage. Summarized on this site's Long Range Plan page.

The plan is the source for the five phases and their target years, the road transition, the administrative cost figures, the $54.25 sewer-loan reduction, the $50–$56 monthly public safety fee estimate, and what the November 3 sales tax vote decides.

Official City sources

  • "Long Range Plan Now Available: Your Input Is Needed" — the City's announcement of the plan and the October 2026 public input period.
  • "Moving the City Forward" — August 2026 post on the 4-mill property tax, the proposed sales tax and the road transfer.
  • January 2026 State of the City — source for the City's first property tax levy of 4 mills, for collection in 2026.
  • "Can the City Do It All?" — A Feasibility Study, presented by Mayor Kees on May 2, 2026 (updated May 6, 2026). An earlier exploration; now historical. Posted on the City website under About/Contact → Services.
  • "The Future Holiday Island — Session 2: Protecting Our Citizens," presented by Mayor Kees on July 11, 2026 (revised July 13, 2026). The earlier police and fire discussion; now historical.

Both presentations were delivered in public forums. The May study is posted on the City's Services page (About/Contact → Services). They remain the only public source for many detailed estimates: the budget tables, the police department cost model, the Urban Service District fee estimates, and the $502,660 sales tax revenue estimate. Wherever this site uses them, they are labeled as earlier feasibility-study estimates. They are not adopted rates.

Site analysis and recommendations

Some of this site is the author's own work, not official City or HISID policy. It is labeled where it appears:

  • The Growth Plan — after its first section, which reports what the Long Range Plan says, the page is the author's independent recommendations on attracting residents, business and healthcare.
  • The Tax Comparison — independently researched sales tax and property tax rates for nearby towns and regional cities, pulled from public rate-lookup services and property-tax data aggregators.
  • The Cost Calculator — a planning tool built on the earlier feasibility-study figures, adjusted to count only the one mill above the 4 the City already levies. Its fixed-income note draws on Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration material on property tax relief.
  • The short-term rental note on the Funding page — from City ordinances and Arkansas's Advertising & Promotion Commission statute.
  • Passages marked "this site's view," such as the one on the Timeline page.

How figures were checked

Statements about the Long Range Plan were checked against the text of the plan itself, and direct quotations are shown in quotation marks. Dollar figures, statistics and formulas attributed to the feasibility study were transcribed from the two presentations. Where a table in an original document could not be reconstructed with full confidence (for example, due to how numbers were laid out on a slide or page), this site either omits that specific figure or summarizes it rather than guessing. Where sources give different numbers for the same thing, this site uses the most recent official figure and says so. Two examples: the plan counts 69 miles of road where the May study counted 71, and the plan's $50–$56 monthly public safety fee range brackets the July presentation's $632-a-year estimate.

Where the Long Range Plan does not specify something, such as an exact future assessment reduction, the Parks and Recreation fee, police staffing, or a final date for HISID's phase-out, this site says it is not yet determined.

A note on point of view

This site's author is a Holiday Island resident who supports moving forward with this transition, and several pages make that case directly. The aim is to keep official facts, independent analysis and recommendations visibly separate, so that a reader who disagrees with the author can still rely on the facts. The one deliberately neutral page is the Public Safety page, covering the still-undecided choice between a sheriff contract and a new city police department.

Questions or corrections

If you believe a figure on this site is inaccurate or out of date, we want to know. Please raise it at a City Council or HISID Board of Commissioners meeting, or reach out through the City's official contact channels at cityofholidayisland.com. Official plans, budgets, tax rates, assessments, petitions, votes and legal actions may change after this site was last updated in October 2026.
