Options
1. Per A.C.A. Annotated §14-52-102 a city of the second class may establish a police department.
2. Per A.C.A. Annotated §14-52-101 a city of the first class shall establish a police department.
3. But, per the statutes, the governing body of the city (1st or 2nd class) can enter into an agreement for providing law enforcement services with an existing enforcement agency in lieu of establishing a police department. In our case, the sheriff department or Eureka Springs police department.
4. Per A.C.A. Annotated §14-52-103 a city of the second class may establish by ordinance either a police department or a city marshal's office. This is not an option for cities of the first class.
What is NOT an option is doing nothing. A second-class city in Arkansas can choose to let the county sheriff handle law enforcement instead of creating its own police department, as long as it formally contracts or agrees with the sheriff's office to provide those services.

Current Situation
· Currently the City contracts with the Carroll County Sheriff Department to provide two full time deputies or the equivalent 80 hours per week to patrol Holiday Island.
· Due to staffing shortages or other reasons, the county can typically only provide 55 to 70 hours per week.
· The city pays the sheriff department $31.28/hr. for the patrol hours reported. The annual cost to the city for two full time deputies is $130,125.
· The current contract expires at the end of 2026. A new contract, provided the County is willing to engage in another contract, is almost certain to increase the cost.

Nine Reasons Why Holiday Island Might Choose a Police Department Over the Deputy Contract.
Jurisdiction and Service Needs
A police department's authority extends to the city's boundaries. Officers patrol streets, respond to 911 calls, and investigate crimes within the city. A sheriff's authority covers the entire county, including rural areas, unincorporated towns, and county facilities. Since a city's needs are patrolling urban or suburban streets and local crime, it may prefer a dedicated department for faster, more targeted response.

Cost and Resource Allocation
While contracting with the sheriff will save money, there is value in creating and maintaining our own department. This can include direct control over budgets, staffing, and equipment. But most importantly avoiding the need to share resources with the county. Under today's arrangement with the county, the needs of the county take priority which historically means that the city does not get the desired number of patrol hours.

Cost vs Coverage
Contracting for 80 hours per week of patrol, even when the county can provide the full contracted hours, leaves the city dependent on the county for response to 911 calls over 50% of the time. If a deputy is not on duty in Holiday Island and a 911 response is needed, the nearest deputy could be 40 miles away and possibly tending to another emergency thus preventing him/her from responding.

Community Control
A police chief is appointed by the city's governing body (city council), giving the city more direct control over hiring, policies, and community relations. This can be important for maintaining local priorities and accountability to residents.

Specialized or Unique Needs
Holiday Island has the unique challenge of having 71 miles of road with dozens of isolated homes. The isolated nature of these areas makes those residents more vulnerable to crime, the properties more likely to house criminal activity, and harder to get to in case of an emergency. Having the situational awareness that comes with a dedicated permanent police force would be an advantage.

Traffic/ Crime Prevention
Writing a speeding ticket now and again is a great deterrent to speeding. But knowing that your chances of getting caught are slim to none is also an incentive to ignore the speed limits. Having more staff doing more patrolling significantly reduces the incentive to speed.
The same can be said for criminal activity. Holiday Island cannot become known as a safe haven for criminal activity.

Revenue
While a city should not use fines as a source of revenue to operate the city, revenue from fines is a proper way for the cost of providing law enforcement to be borne by those breaking the law. At least in part. Today any fine revenue generated from traffic violations or criminal prosecution goes to the county. If we had a police department, that revenue would come back to the city.

Assistance with Code Enforcement
By law the sheriff's department cannot enforce city ordinances. Code enforcement officers can at times be operating in potentially dangerous areas with potentially dangerous people. A police officer could assist in code enforcement and are trained and equipped to operate in these types of potentially dangerous situations.

Community Connection and Involvement
Historically, the Holiday Island community has had a great relationship with the fire department. The fire chief has always been well known and liked in the community as are the fire fighters. And even though the deputies that serve Holiday Island are great talented professionals as is Sheriff Klatt, it is not likely that the sheriff's department can develop the same community connection that a stable police force would.

In summary, there are many benefits to Holiday Island having its own police department. But it comes with a price tag. The community must make a decision if they want to take that next step.

The Marshal Option A.C.A. Annotated §14-52-103
(a) The marshal of cities of the second class shall execute and return all writs and process directed to him or her by the mayor. In criminal cases or cases of a violation of the city ordinance, he or she may serve them in any part of the county. (b) It shall be the marshal's duty to:
· (1) Suppress all riots and disturbances and breaches of the peace; · (2) Apprehend all disorderly persons in the city; · (3) Pursue and arrest any person fleeing from justice in any part of the state; and · (4) Apprehend any person in the act of committing any offense against the laws of the
state or ordinances of the city and forthwith to bring such persons before the mayor, or other competent authority, for examination or trial.
· (c) The marshal shall have power to appoint one (1) or more deputies, for whose official acts he or she shall be responsible.
· (d) In the discharge of his or her proper duties, the marshal shall have like powers, be subject to like responsibilities, and receive the like fees as sheriffs and constables in similar cases.

Cost of Marshal vs. Police
Benefits for police officers in Arkansas are essentially established by State Statute (Title 14 Chapter 52). Benefits for Marshals and subordinate officers are set by the city council. This can reduce operating cost assuming it does not hinder recruitment. But in order to have the authority to enforce criminal and traffic laws, they would have to be a law enforcement officer. Which means they would have to go through police academy training and be CLEST certified and the city would bear the cost just the same as for a police officer.

Prevalence of Marshals in Arkansas
· A quick search found only one city of the 2nd class that had a marshal and they also have a police department.
· Six incorporated towns with populations from 94 to 808 have marshals.
Basically, nobody uses a Marshal's office as an alternative to a police department or working with the sheriff department.

Interlocal Agreement with Eureka Springs Option
An agreement similar to the county agreement is legally possible. However, there are some important differences.
· The sheriff has an incentive to work cooperatively with Holiday Island in that it is an elected position and Holiday Island residents, vote. Plus, the sheriff already has county wide responsibility.
· The electors of Holiday Island have no voice in who the mayor is for Eureka Springs and as such, will likely have no say in who is assigned to Holiday Island. The Eureka Springs mayor's first responsibity is to the people of Eureka Springs.
· How well the interlocal agreement works will likely be very dependent on who is sitting in the respective mayor's chairs at any given time.
Neither the County nor Eureka Springs are obligated to enter into an interlocal agreement.

Holiday Island is likely in this range
Why? Sales tax dollars go to the cities where people are spending their money.

Why the Difference?
Most cities grow organically. · In "organic" cities the population is primarily hometown residents
who are raising their families there, doing their shopping there, eating at their restaurants, sending their kids to school there, buying most everything they need there. And are possibly the hub of a rural area. Horseshoe Bend and Cherokee Village on the other hand, like Holiday Island, were created by developers for retirement living.
· The population is primarily retired. · All three cities are within 20 minutes of a Walmart in another city
and within an hour from a major city where the residents go and spend their money. They only buy the necessities locally or online. · Some only live in their Holiday Island home part time.
· Holiday Island has the added disadvantage of being 5 miles from a tourist town with over 70 places to eat and be entertained.

Holiday Island Sales Tax Potential
· The 2020 census population for Holiday Island is 2399. However more recent population estimates put the population at 2452. That would seem to be a reasonable number to use for estimating sales tax potential.
· Using a population of 2452 and a sales tax yield of $82.00/person per percent of tax, a 2.5% sales tax would yield $502,660.

Why do we need an Urban Service District with a fee when other cities don't? Reason #2
Holiday Island's atypical Fire Department Cost Structure

Fire Department - Background
· Fire department budgets for a city of 2500 typically runs $250,000 on the high side vs Holiday Island @ $574,081.
· The current operating budget for the fire department is felt to be justifiable even though it may be more than most cities our size due to how spread-out Holiday Island is, the number of residents requiring more medical assistance, and the difficulty getting volunteers due to most of the population being retired.
· The current capital reserve to replace trucks and maintain four fire stations is inadequate.

Next Step - The Petition Process
· The police department, along with the fire department, would be funded by a monthly Urban Service District fee added to the water bill.
· An Urban Service District can be established by ordinance by the city council upon being presented with a petition from the qualified electors (registered voters) of Holiday Island.
· Signatures equaling a minimum of 25% of the Holiday Island electors that voted in the last election for governor is required which is 325 signatures.

How Do We Decide Police or Deputies?
· Circulate a petition to form an Urban Service District to include a police department and the fire department.
· Signing the petition would constitute a vote for the police department. If the petition fails to get the required signatures, another petition would be circulated to form an Urban Service District authorizing a new interlocal agreement with the Sheriff Department.
· If neither petition is successful, the transfer of the fire department to the city would be put on hold.
