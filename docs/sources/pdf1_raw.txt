Prospects for Suburban Improvement Districts
· Improvement districts are authorized under various Arkansas statues initiated by Act 41 of 1941.
· Purpose - to provide a mechanism for funding the creation of infrastructure like roads and sewer and water works primarily in rural Arkansas.
· Since 1970, Holiday Island has operated as a Suburban Improvement District formed under Ark Code Annotated §14-92-201 et. seq.
· The statute focuses primarily on recovery of investment and provides no good mechanism for funding long-term maintenance and operation.
· Attempts by Suburban and Municipal Recreational Improvement Districts to deal with the problem have resulted in lawsuits.

Current Examples
· As a result of a lawsuit, Horseshoe Bend has already dissolved their improvement district with the city taking over the recreational amenities in 2025.
· Ozark Acres settled a lawsuit by considering incorporating and phasing out the SID by 2028.
· A current judge's order in a Cherokee Village lawsuit has the SID being dissolved by the end of 2026.
· Holiday Island settled one lawsuit in 2011 and is now involved in another lawsuit that still must be litigated.
· None of these districts intended to illegally exact a tax from the property owners. They simply tried to find a way to pay the bills. Nevertheless, the lawsuits ensued.

Thinking Out Loud
Isn't it prudent then for Holiday Island to be thinking about the possibility of a fate similar to the other Improvement Districts?

Roads - Background
· Holiday Island has 71 miles of roads. Put end to end, they would reach to Springfield, Mo. going north or to the Oklahoma border at Siloam Springs going west.
· Typical cities our size have 10 miles of streets which would only get you halfway to Berryville.
· Most city streets are concrete or asphalt with curb and gutter which requires resurfacing on a 25-30 year cycle with zero ditch maintenance expense.
· Only 40% of Holiday Island roads are concrete or asphalt. The remainder of the roads are chip and seal which requires resurfacing every 5-15 years depending on how good the road base is.
· Almost all the roads have ditches that require maintenance. 71 miles of road increases other maintenance cost proportionally like snow removal.
· The city received one $300,000 grant for paving and has been awarded another $400,000 grant that we will receive in 2026 or 2027. However, grant money is not guaranteed.

Sewer and Water - Background
· Just like the roads, Holiday Island has over 71 miles of sewer and water lines to maintain.
· The current treatment plant bond matures in October of 2031 but can likely be paid off within the next 18 months.
· New legislation mandates that cities include establishing and maintaining a capital replacement fund to cover the inevitable cost to replace water lines, pumps, water tanks, treatment plants and the like.

Revenue Sources
Cities in Arkansas have limited sources of revenue. · Sales tax which must be approved by voters · Property tax limited to 5 mills ($.005 x 20% of assessed
property value) · Fuel tax turnback funds from the State for road maintenance · Franchise fees (limited to 4.25% of taxable revenue) · Fees for services provided (water sales, sanitation fees, use of
recreational facilities, etc.) · Special purpose district fees (Urban Service Districts)

Sales Taxes
· Cities in Arkansas generate much of their revenue, generally well over half, from sales tax.
· The next slide shows sales tax revenue for several small cities in Arkansas.
· As you will see, the average annual revenue per person per 1% of tax is $239.

Cherokee Village and Horseshoe Bend
But Cherokee Village and Horseshoe Bend are significantly less at only about $80 per person per percent of tax.
Holiday Island is likely in this range
Why? Sales tax dollars go to the cities where people are spending their money.

Why the Difference?
Most cities grow organically. · In "organic" cities the population is primarily hometown residents
who are raising their families there, doing their shopping there, eating at their restaurants, sending their kids to school there, buying most everything they need there. And are possibly the hub of a rural area. Horseshoe Bend and Cherokee Village on the other hand, like Holiday Island, were created by developers for retirement living.
· The population is primarily retired. · All three cities are within 20 minutes of a Walmart in another city
and within an hour from a major city where the residents go and spend their money. They only buy the necessities locally or online. · Some only live in their Holiday Island home part time.
· Holiday Island has the added disadvantage of being 5 miles from a tourist town with over 70 places to eat and be entertained.

Property Tax
· With a couple of very specific exceptions, the Arkansas constitution limits the amount a city can levy in property tax to 5 mills.
· The millage is assessed against 20% of what the county Assessor determines to be the value of your real estate and personal property.
· Example: Your home and lot are assessed to be worth $250,000. And you have two automobiles valued at $40,000. Your 5 mill city property tax would be $250,000 + $40,000 x 20% x .005 or $290.00.

County Property Tax for Roads
· The County levies a 3 mill property tax for roads on all property in the county. Including property owners who live in a city.
· 50% of that tax collected on property that is within a city is returned to that city but can only be used for street/road maintenance.
· The County shows this on your tax bill as a 1.5 mill "city" road tax.

Turnback Funds
· The State receives revenue for road and highway construction and maintenance from several sources. But the majority comes from the fuel tax paid at the retail level (at the gas pump) or wholesale level.
· A portion of that revenue is returned to the cities on a per capita basis.
· Each city gets the same amount per resident based on the most recent census.
· Only people that claim Holiday Island as their permanent residence are included in the census population.

Franchise Fees
· Arkansas statutes allow for cities to charge a franchise fee typically for companies using the roads or road rights of way to provide their service.
· The statute limits the fee to 4.25% of taxable revenue unless a larger percentage is agreed to by both parties.
· Most cities in Arkansas charge franchise fees. · In Holiday Island, candidates for franchise fees would be
electricity, cable TV, trash service. · The statutes also allow for a city to charge a franchise fee on
water sales (but not sewer) even if provided by the city.

Fees for Service
· Cities can collect revenue for services they provide. Examples would include sewer and water and sanitation fees (trash pick up).
· Cities can also set up special purpose districts and charge a fee. Examples include a Public Improvement District (PID) to upgrade public spaces or facilities, a Municipal Utility District (MUD) to provide an essential service, a Community Development District for economic development, or an Urban Service District to provide an essential service.

Parks and Recreation Urban Service District Remember this is a STUDY. Not a Proposal!
Providing the current recreational amenities requires a $495,035 annual subsidy from assessment revenue. Consequently, this study makes some assumptions.
· A Parks and Recreation Urban Service District would be established. · The 9-hole golf course would be converted into a large mowed park area. Grants
would be solicited to repurpose the area into lower maintenance activities. · The swimming pool would become a public pool requiring lifeguards. Use of the
pool would be free for USD members. The public would pay to use the pool. · The Clubhouse would become a community center. Use of the community
center would be free for USD paying members for community functions. · Golf would continue to carry a greens fee for anyone to play. Without the 9-hole
maintenance expense, focus can be put on improving the quality of the 18-hole course thereby eventually increasing play and revenue. · Since all property owners would be able to use the USD amenities, all property owners would be billed the annual fee.

Parks and Recreation Nationally
· The national average for spending on parks and recreation for a city of 2500 population is $81.19/capita. Or $203,000 annual. This would be for the type of parks and recreation you would find in a typical small town.
· Holiday Island is primarily a vacation and retirement community where above average recreational amenities would be a key element for growth and sustainability.
· Lifestyle is also a key consideration for families looking for a place to work from home and raise a family.
