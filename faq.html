Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions residents are most likely to have.

"My assessment is only $835 a year now, and I don't pay city sales tax or property tax. Why would I want this?"

That's a completely fair reaction, and the study addresses it directly. HISID currently collects about $2.3 million a year in assessment revenue (net of the sewer bond). If HISID's ability to collect that assessment is ever successfully challenged — as has already happened to Horseshoe Bend, and is happening now to Cherokee Village — that $2.3 million has to be replaced by something, on a timeline nobody in Holiday Island gets to choose. Planning the replacement now, deliberately, is what keeps this from becoming an emergency later. See your own numbers with the Cost Calculator.

I'm retired and on a fixed income. Is there any relief built into this plan?

Some, yes. The new city property tax — unlike today's flat HISID assessment — qualifies for Arkansas's Homestead Tax Credit (recently raised to roughly $600 a year) and, if you're 65 or older or disabled, a freeze on your home's taxable value so it never rises again. The Recreation and Public Safety fees don't have an income-based discount, and that's worth saying plainly. See the full, honest picture — including what isn't covered — in the Cost Calculator's note for fixed-income residents.

Why do vacant lots see costs go down while improved properties see them go up?

Vacant lots currently generate about $840,000 of HISID's $2.3 million in assessment revenue — but under a 5-mill property tax, those same lots would generate only about $33,000. Replacing a flat per-lot assessment with property tax and usage-based fees necessarily shifts more of the funding responsibility onto improved properties and the residents who directly use city services like water, recreation, and public safety.

Is this actually decided already?

No. As of this writing, this is a feasibility study, not an adopted plan. Nothing changes in your bill unless a sales-tax ballot measure passes and enough residents sign the required petitions. See the full timeline for exactly what has to happen, in what order.

What happens if the petitions or the sales-tax vote fail?

Each stage of the plan has a built-in off-ramp. If the November sales-tax vote fails, further transition planning is put on hold. If the Public Safety Urban Service District petition fails to reach 325 signatures, that transition — and the fire department transition alongside it — is put on hold. If the Parks & Recreation USD petition fails, that transition is put on hold too. Nothing here is a one-way door. See why the sales-tax vote specifically carries the most weight of any single step in this timeline.

Why does a "growth plan" matter for a transition that's mostly about paying today's bills?

Because the same revenue tools that fund the transition — sales tax, property tax on improved value, and Urban Service District fees — all grow automatically as Holiday Island grows, without another rate increase. A single vacant lot becoming a home, or one more family choosing to move here, adds directly to that base. See the Growth Plan page for how that works and why it depends on passing the November vote first.

Even with a new sales tax and property tax, is Holiday Island still an affordable place to live?

Yes, based on independent research into nearby towns and larger cities. Holiday Island's estimated combined tax bill after a full transition lands right in the middle of comparable Arkansas towns like Cherokee Village, Berryville, Green Forest, and Eureka Springs — and roughly 20% to 40% below Dallas, Fort Worth, Chicago, and Kansas City on the same illustrative household. See the full breakdown, with sources, on the Tax Comparison page.

Why can't the city just raise property tax instead of creating new fees?

The Arkansas constitution caps city property tax at 5 mills — a hard ceiling, not a policy choice the City Council can adjust. That's why the plan relies on a mix of sources (sales tax, the existing 5-mill cap, fuel-tax turnback, franchise fees, and new Urban Service District fees) rather than a single property tax increase.

Is the police department decision part of this site's overall recommendation?

No — deliberately not. This site advocates for the overall transition from HISID to the city, but the sheriff-contract-vs-police-department question is treated as a genuinely open community decision. Read the neutral, side-by-side comparison on the Public Safety page.

Where do these numbers come from?

Every figure on this site is drawn from two public presentations delivered by Mayor Kees: a feasibility study on May 2, 2026 (updated May 6), and a follow-up "Session 2" focused on police and fire protection on July 11, 2026 (revised July 13). See About This Site for full sourcing.
