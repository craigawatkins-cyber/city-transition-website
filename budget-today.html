Where the money goes today

Right now, two separate governments run Holiday Island side by side: the City, and HISID. Add them together, and this small lake town already spends more than most Arkansas cities its size.

$4,274,877HISID operating budget1

Source: 2026 budgets as presented in the May 2, 2026 feasibility study. This is the "before" picture. The September 2026 Long Range Plan describes how these responsibilities would move to the City in five phases, starting with roads in 2027.

Administration is the line the Long Range Plan focuses on. It puts current administrative expense at $203,087 for the City and $890,343 for HISID, $1,093,430 combined, and projects a single long-term administration at about $640,000 once HISID is phased out2. See How We'd Pay.

Why we run higher — three honest reasons

We're spread out

Holiday Island maintains 69 miles of roads. A typical city our size has 5 to 102. More road miles means more paving, more ditches, more snow removal, more of everything, spread across a much larger footprint per resident.

We're an older community

Most residents are retired or of retirement age, which means a higher share of medical and EMS calls, and a harder time recruiting volunteer firefighters — both of which push public safety costs up.

We invest in recreation

Two golf courses, a marina, pools, trails, and a community center are a big part of why people choose to live here — and a big part of the budget. It's a deliberate above-average investment, not an accident.

A bigger city budget doesn't automatically mean a bigger tax bill on any one household — see how Holiday Island's actual sales tax and property tax rates would stack up against these same peer towns, plus Dallas–Fort Worth, Chicago, and Kansas City, on the Tax Comparison page.
